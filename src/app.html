04 — The micro-trends

One level below the themes

A theme is a sector; a niche is a bet. "Physical AI & Robotics" contains robot foundation models, humanoid hardware, warehouse automation and surgical robotics — different physics, different timelines, different odds. Averaging them produces one bland number and buries every actual signal.

So the same six-factor emergence score runs again here, on — specific niches instead of sixteen sectors. Each also carries a lifecycle phase computed from two things: how concentrated the capital is (has a winner emerged?) and whether funding accelerated or decelerated year on year.

06 — Ways in

What of this you can actually buy

Every theme mapped to the listed instruments that carry business exposure to it — pure-plays first, then diluted proxies, then funds. The proxy quality score rates how faithfully public markets track the private thesis on this board, from 5 (you can essentially buy it) down to 0 (no listed representation exists).

Read the scores against the Horizon view and the pattern is hard to miss: the themes that score highest on emergence are the ones you can least buy. That is not a coincidence — absence of a public proxy is one of the six emergence factors. Investability is what maturity looks like from the outside.

Signal table — the derivatives

Totals say where money is. These say which way it is moving. Momentum is trailing-twelve-month capital against the twelve months before. Acceleration compares that with the same reading six months ago — a theme can still be growing while it slows. Cadence is the median gap between a company's consecutive rounds: when it shortens, investors are competing to get back in. Click a column to sort, a theme to filter the tape.

Round tape

Every dated round on the board, newest first. Step is the round's size against the same company's previous round — a 3× step means the next cheque was three times the last one.

How this was built. Positions are compiled from public reporting on funding rounds, family-office disclosures and company announcements, researched 4 August 2026. A "position" means an operator, their fund, family office or corporate venture arm is a disclosed backer — it does not imply stake size, and undisclosed positions are by definition missing, so the board understates quiet operators and overstates loud ones. Valuations are last known post-money and go stale fast; several are marked reported where a round was in negotiation rather than closed. Ventures that have since listed appear only in the graduated strip, since the brief was private companies. Company marks are procedurally generated from each name, not official logos.

On the involvement layer. Roles — chief executive, co-founder, chairman, board seat, advisor — are sourced from company leadership pages, appointment announcements and reporting. This data is materially sparser and harder to verify than funding data, so the layer understates involvement: a position showing as capital-only means no role was found, not that none exists. Advisory-board seats in particular are rarely announced and are almost certainly under-represented here. Two listed companies, Oklo and Palantir, are included despite the private-company brief precisely because the role is the signal — Altman chaired Oklo from Y Combinator to the NYSE before stepping down in April 2025, and Thiel has chaired Palantir since 2003, which is where most of the defence cluster on this board originates.

How emergence is scored. Six factors, equally directional, weighted: stage centroid (20%), median valuation (20%), share of capital raised in the trailing twelve months (20%), founder-built share of positions (15%), absence of crossover and sovereign investors (15%), and absence of a listed pure-play comparable (10%). Each is normalised to 0–100 where higher means earlier. It is a heuristic for ordering themes against each other, not a forecast — a high score says the market has not yet repriced a theme, not that it will. Themes carrying fewer than four ventures are flagged with ⚠ in the factor table: with that few companies a single outlier moves the score several points, so treat those rankings as directional at best.

On the non-US entries. Valuations outside North America are disclosed far less often, are frequently state-influenced, and in several cases are pre-marketing targets rather than priced rounds. Every such figure carries a confidence grade and a caveat on its card; eleven entities are flagged as state programmes, self-funded companies or SPAC announcements rather than venture-backed companies, and should never be read as priced valuations. Legal domicile is shown separately from operational headquarters where they diverge, because a Delaware flip is close to mandatory for any Latin American company raising a US round — a dataset keyed on incorporation undercounts those regions, and one keyed on founder nationality overcounts them.

Why regions are not comparable on value alone. China's most valuable private companies listed in 2025–26 and therefore left this board; Africa and Latin America disclose almost nothing; Gulf entities are government budgets rather than equity. Comparing tracked value across regions measures disclosure practice as much as it measures activity. Venture counts and emergence scores travel better than dollar totals.
