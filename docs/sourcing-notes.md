### Why the gap is real
- Both regions raised **$4.1B total in 2025** — all sectors, all stages.
  Africa's was **41% debt**. African deep tech: ~$3B cumulatively 2013–2023.
- **No anchor public customer** — no DARPA/SBIR/ESA/EIC equivalent. A private
  LatAm launch or defence company has no procurement path.
- LatAm: no domestic growth capital for science (pension funds absent), high
  Brazilian risk-free rates, Argentine FX controls, no pre-revenue listings on
  B3 or Santiago. Science base is good (Embrapa, USP, CONICET); spinout
  machinery is missing.
- Africa: **the missing middle** — grants and DFI seed available, Series B/C
  absent exactly where capital intensity peaks. Currency devaluation destroys
  dollar returns on hardware. Much capital is DFI/concessional (BII, Norfund,
  IFC, DFC, Acumen) — patient, but structured differently and it does not
  produce Series C step-ups.
- **54gene cautionary case**: raised $45M for African genomics, three CEOs in a
  year, wound down 2023, biobank of ~100,000 Nigerian genomes later offered for
  ~$3M before a Lagos court froze the sale in 2025. Made generalist investors
  materially more cautious about African life sciences.
- **Mexico and Colombia have essentially nothing in deep tech.** Mexico's 2026
  surge ($1.68B through July, overtaking Brazil for the first time in 15 years)
  is fintech, proptech, logistics. If a dataset shows Mexico as a deep-tech
  market, that is an artefact.
- **Brazil has no venture-backed orbital launch company.** MLBR is a
  state-led consortium around AEB. Innospace (Korean) launches *from* Alcântara
  — that is Brazilian infrastructure, not a Brazilian company.
